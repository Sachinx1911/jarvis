# What personal-AI-assistant features real users use and value (2025 to Oct 2026)

Scope note: research done 2026-10-10. Most usage numbers come from vendor-published studies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft), which are large but self-reported. Independent surveys (Pew, Reuters Institute, Menlo/Morning Consult, Riskified, Accenture) are smaller but neutral. Several figures came through secondary summaries; they are marked "(secondary)".

## 1. What do people actually use AI assistants for every day? (usage studies)

### Takeaway
Daily use is dominated by three things: asking questions / getting advice, information lookup (search replacement), and writing help (mostly editing text the user already has). Most consumer use is non-work. Health and personal advice are rising, especially on phones. "Doing things for me" (agents) is a small, newer slice.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI/NBER "How People Use ChatGPT" (NBER WP 34255, Sept 2025, consumer plans, data to mid-2025): "Practical Guidance", "Seeking Information" and "Writing" together are nearly 80% of conversations. Practical Guidance is about 28.8%. Seeking Information grew from 14% to 24%. Within writing, editing/critiquing user-provided text (10.6%) beats personal writing such as emails (8%). — [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255); numbers via [TechSpot](https://techspot.com/news/109483-openai-reveals-how-people-really-use-chatgpt-personal.html) (secondary)
- Same paper: non-work share rose from 53% (June 2024) to 73% (June 2025). Authors say the main value is "decision support". — [TechSpot](https://techspot.com/news/109483-openai-reveals-how-people-really-use-chatgpt-personal.html) (secondary); [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255)
- Gap note: the paper's Asking / Doing / Expressing split (roughly 49% / 40% / 11%) and small shares for coding and companionship are widely quoted, but I could not open OpenAI's page to re-check them (HTTP 403). Verify in the NBER PDF before quoting. — [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255)
- Anthropic Economic Index, Jan 2026 (Nov 2025 data): Claude.ai use split about 46% work, 35% personal, 19% coursework (secondary summary). Personal use is spreading beyond power users in richer countries. — [Anthropic Jan 2026 report](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report); split via [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@ZombieCodeKill/claude-reviews-anthropics-economic-index-ed44052c6e12) (secondary)
- Anthropic Economic Index, March 2026 (1M conversations, 5 to 12 Feb 2026): users with 6+ months of tenure succeed in conversations at a measurably higher rate than new users, so skill with the tool matters. — [ppc.land summary](https://ppc.land/experienced-claude-users-succeed-10-more-and-the-gap-is-widening/) (secondary)
- Anthropic "Cadences" report (June 2026, telemetry plus a survey of about 9,700 users): personal queries are about 35% on weekdays and nearly 50% on weekends (secondary, not checked against the original). — [noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/en/news/anthropic-economic-index-cadences-ai-work-patterns-2026) (secondary)
- Microsoft Copilot Usage Report 2025 (37.5M de-identified consumer conversations, Jan to Sep 2025): health is the #1 mobile topic in every month and at every time of day. Programming peaks on weekdays and games on weekends. Travel peaks at commute hours. Search is still the most used feature, but requests for personal advice are rising. — [Microsoft AI](https://microsoft.ai/news/its-about-time-the-copilot-usage-report-2025/)
- Microsoft follow-up (Jan 2026, 500k+ health conversations): in nearly 1 in 5, people describe their own symptoms, read their own test results or manage their own conditions. Many ask on behalf of family members. — [Microsoft AI health](https://microsoft.ai/health/) (via search summary)
- Reuters Institute Generative AI and News Report 2025 (YouGov, about 2,000 people per country in AR, DK, FR, JP, UK, US; June to July 2025): weekly use of any gen-AI tool went from 18% to 34%. Weekly use for getting information went from 11% to 24%, now the top use. AI for news is only 6%. 22% used ChatGPT in the last week. Ages 18 to 24: 59% weekly; 55+: 20%. — [Reuters Institute PDF](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Gen_AI_and_News_Report_2025.pdf); [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/10/people-are-using-chatgpt-twice-as-much-as-they-were-last-year-theyre-still-just-as-skeptical-of-ai-in-news/)
- Pew (US, fieldwork about 17 to 23 Feb, secondary): 49% of US adults use chatbots (33% in 2024), about a quarter daily. Uses: searching for information 42%, work tasks 38% of workers, about a fifth for medical or diet/fitness info, under 15% for news, 10% for emotional support. 30% say chatbots help productivity, 5% say they hurt. — [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/americans-use-ai-more-than-they-trust-it/); [syndicated news report](https://weartv.com/news/nation-world/americans-embrace-ai-but-with-skeptical-eye-pew-survey-shows) (secondary; Pew's own page not reached, year presumed 2026)
- Menlo Ventures State of Consumer AI 2025 (Morning Consult, 5,031 US adults, April 2025): 61% used AI in the past 6 months, nearly 1 in 5 daily. Only 3% pay. Most use is quick, simple tasks. — [Menlo 2025](https://www.menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- Menlo 2026 report (Sept 2026; Morning Consult, about 5,067 US adults, July 2026 per a secondary source): adult AI use went only from 61% to 64%. — [Menlo 2026 PDF](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/menlo_ventures_consumer_ai_report-2026.pdf)

#### India
- OpenAI "How India uses ChatGPT" (Signals, Feb 2026): India has 100M+ weekly users. Technical tasks are nearly 4x the global average. Codex use is about 3x. About 3x more coding questions and about 2x more education questions than the global median. Work share is about 35% (about 30% globally). Non-work messages: about 35% practical guidance, about 20% each for information and writing. Ages 18 to 24 send just under half of messages; 18 to 34 send about 80%. — [OpenAI PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/signals/how-india-uses-chatgpt.pdf); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/indias-chatgpt-use-for-technical-tasks-nearly-4x-global-average-says-openai-90342.htm); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/india-leads-global-chatgpt-use-for-coding-data-analysis-says-openai-enn26022004757)
- The top 10 Indian cities are about 50% of usage but under 10% of the population (secondary report based on OpenAI data). — [India AI Edge report](https://nas.com/ai-champions/digital-files/the-india-ai-edge-report-2026) (secondary)
- LocalCircles (15,377 respondents, Aug 2024 to Feb 2025, older data): 1 in 2 Indian internet users use AI. 28% use ChatGPT to find information. 90% use text mode, 10% voice. 31% willing to switch to DeepSeek. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/start-up/chatgpt-most-used-ai-platform-to-find-info-in-india-31-willing-to-switch-to-deepseek-survey)

### Inferences
- For a one-user assistant, the core daily loop is "ask / decide / look up / edit my text". It should be excellent and fast before agentic features get attention.
- Health and family questions are a big, sensitive daily use. They need good source-backed answers and careful privacy.
- Indian users (like Sachin) lean more technical and learning-heavy than the global average. Coding and learning help likely matter more here.
- Voice was a minority mode in India (10%, older data). Voice is useful but probably not the main daily channel.

### Gaps
- No public 2026 breakdown of Indian AI use by language (Hindi, Marathi and others) or by voice share. OpenAI's India PDF may have it, but I could not read past the opening pages.
- Google Gemini has published no usage-category study comparable to the OpenAI and Microsoft ones (none found).
- Pew's own report page was not reached. Its privacy findings are unknown.

## 2. Which features drive daily retention (memory, proactive briefs, reminders, voice, email, research, shopping/booking, computer use)?

### Takeaway
There is no public retention data per feature. The indirect evidence: agents are used regularly by a minority of AI users (about a quarter), mostly paying heavy users. Research and recommendations are trusted far more than transactions. Computer-use agents now beat the human baseline on OSWorld-Verified, but booking and shopping on real sites still often fail (about 17% to 35% on realistic benchmarks).

### Cited Findings
- Menlo 2026: among AI users, 41% have tried an AI agent, 24% use one regularly, and 32% have let AI act for them without final approval. Among agent users, 92% pay and 63% use AI daily. Payers are 5x as likely to use agents (65% vs 13%) and nearly 2x as likely to use AI daily (50% vs 26%). — [Menlo 2026 PDF](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/menlo_ventures_consumer_ai_report-2026.pdf); via [Selfstorming](https://www.selfstorming.com/blog/ai-in-marketing/consumers-started-paying-for-ai) (secondary)
- About 36% have given an agent access to their email and 20% to a financial account (single secondary blog; unverified). — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-agent-access-permissions-consumer-survey-statistics-2026) (secondary)
- ChatGPT Pulse (proactive daily brief, launched Sept 2025 for Pro, mobile only): Sam Altman called it his favourite feature. Early reception was mixed: useful for morning triage when the user steers it, but some found proactive notifications intrusive, and privacy concerns dominated discussion. No usage or retention data found. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-25/chatgpt-feature-offers-users-a-personalized-daily-briefing); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2025/09/26/chatgpt-launches-new-briefing-feature-called-pulse/); [SentiSight analysis](https://www.sentisight.ai/is-sam-altman-right-about-chatgpt-pulse/) (commentary)
- Memory: a CHI 2026 interview study (20 ChatGPT users) found memory helps "relationship building", but most participants had negative surprises when they saw what was remembered. They asked for visibility, access, transparency and control. — [UGA Grady](https://grady.uga.edu/research/ai-never-forgets-exploring-users-perceptions-of-ai-memory-and-chatgpts-memory-feature/)
- Memory sentiment on X is split between "useful daily" and "unreliable, hallucinates" (about 22 posts, anecdotal). — [Digg](https://digg.com/tech/a1tntmsx)
- OSWorld (computer-use) human baseline is 72.36%. Agent S first passed it in Dec 2025. GPT-5.4 self-reported 75.0% (Mar 2026). A BenchLM snapshot on 22 Aug 2026 shows Qwen3.8 Max at 86.1% and Claude Mythos 5 and Claude Fable 5 at 85%. Another tracker (Oct 2026) lists a top entry at 90.19% (unverified). On the harder OSWorld 2.0, the best entry fully completes only 44.33%. Few results are independently verified by XLANG Lab. — [BenchLM](https://www.benchlm.ai/benchmarks/osworld-verified); [Presenc AI](https://presenc.ai/research/osworld-computer-use-leaderboard-2026); [agentmarketcap](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/10/gpt54-osworld-human-baseline-computer-use-benchmark)
- Booking: on a 2026 BookingArena benchmark, systems reach about 17% to 33% task success. Operator was about 33%. — [arXiv 2602.12544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12544)
- Shopping: on DeepShop (2025), holistic success is typically below 35%. — [arXiv 2506.02839](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02839v1)
- Site design matters: in an AudioEye audit (Sept 2026), agents completed 31% of tasks on an inaccessible retail site and 96% on an accessible version of the same site. — [Agile Brand Guide](https://agilebrandguide.com/yesterdays-martech-ai-cx-news-september-25-2026/)
- Older live-web data (2024): Agent-E had about 27% success on Booking.com, and most failures were not recognised by the agent itself. — [arXiv 2407.13032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.13032) (older)

### Inferences
- Agents are a power-user feature, but those users are also the most engaged ones. Sachin fits that profile.
- Memory is valued only if it is visible and editable. A "what I remember about you" view with edit/delete is a retention feature, not polish.
- Proactive briefs should be opt-in, steerable and quiet (no spammy notifications).
- Use agents for research, comparison and drafting. Keep purchases and bookings as "prepare, then the user confirms". Desktop computer use is much more reliable than real-web booking flows.
- Agents often fail silently, so the assistant must verify its own result (screenshot or read-back) before reporting "done".

### Gaps
- No public per-feature retention or DAU data (Pulse, memory, reminders, email triage, deep research) from any vendor.
- No independent study of email-triage or reminder agents' accuracy for consumers.
- No ChatGPT Agent / Atlas usage numbers. No evidence found either way on whether people abandon agent mode.

## 3. Complaints and failure modes (trust, errors, approval fatigue, privacy, cost)

### Takeaway
The main complaints are: errors and hallucinations that users must catch, agents acting wrongly or failing silently on real sites, prompt-injection and privacy risk when agents work inside logged-in accounts, and too many approval prompts, which users then rubber-stamp. Trust in agent purchases is falling even as use rises.

### Cited Findings
- Riskified (US/UK): in Q1 2026, 55.0% were not comfortable with AI agents making purchases for them, and 46.5% trust no company to manage purchases for them. In Q4 2025, about 7 in 10 were at least somewhat comfortable, so comfort is falling. — [Riskified IR PDF](https://ir.riskified.com/node/8896/pdf); [Forkast](https://forkast.news/73-use-ai-for-shopping-55-wont-let-it-buy-anything-the-trust-gap-is-widening/)
- Accenture Consumer Pulse 2026 (25,590 people, 16 countries): 74% would trust a personal AI agent more than their best friend to buy for them. But only 32% would let it decide what to buy if they still confirm payment, and only 9% accept fully autonomous purchases including payment. — [Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/ma-en/insights/consulting/talk-my-ai-agent)
- RTB House (1,840 consumers, US/UK/FR/JP): 35% want human approval before an AI completes a purchase. — [search summary, RTB House survey](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260427900819/en/) (secondary)
- Prompt injection: a Trail of Bits audit of Perplexity Comet (20 Feb 2026, commissioned by Perplexity) showed four techniques to extract private data from logged-in sessions, including sending Gmail contents to an attacker URL. Brave found an earlier Comet flaw (CVSS 8.6). LayerX showed "Tainted Memories" in ChatGPT Atlas within 24h of launch (a page planting persistent memory instructions). OpenAI's CISO called prompt injection "a frontier, unsolved security problem" (Oct 2025). — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/perplexity-comet-what-an-ai-native-browser-actually-does/); [Delante](https://delante.co/ai-browsers-comet-atlas/); [aisotools Atlas review](https://aisotools.com/blog/chatgpt-atlas-review-2026)
- Approval fatigue: an arXiv paper (2026) reports that users turn on "YOLO mode" (allow everything) rather than answer hundreds of prompts per session. Prompts show commands, not their effects, so approvals are not informed. — [arXiv 2604.13536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.13536)
- The often-quoted "users approve 93% of Claude Code prompts" figure has no traceable primary source. — [Approval fatigue analysis](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/approval-fatigue-agent-permission-ux-2026) (commentary; figure unverified)
- Older analogues: UAC prompts approved about 89% of the time; only about 17% of Android users paid attention to install permissions (Felt et al.). — [grith.ai](https://grith.ai/blog/permission-fatigue-security-failure) (secondary, older)
- Early agent quality: a July 2025 hands-on test had ChatGPT agent take about 40 minutes for a mediocre slide. The same reviewer says it improved a lot by June 2026. — [Zenken](https://ai.zenken.co.jp/en/post/agent-mode/)
- Cost: only 3% of US AI users paid in 2025. Agent use is concentrated among payers (65% of payers vs 13% of non-payers use agents). — [Menlo 2025](https://www.menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-consumer-ai/); [Menlo 2026](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/menlo_ventures_consumer_ai_report-2026.pdf)
- Enterprise signal: Gartner forecasts more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be abandoned by end of 2027 (cost, unclear value, governance). — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/gartner-agentic-ai-projects-cancelled-2027)
- Pew: a plurality of Americans expect AI to have a negative effect on them and society, and younger adults are both the heaviest users and the most negative. — [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/americans-use-ai-more-than-they-trust-it/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Approvals should be risk-tiered: no prompt for reads, batch approval for low-risk steps, and a clear "effect preview" (what will change, where, how much money) for irreversible ones. That reduces prompt count while keeping the dangerous steps gated.
- Treat all web and email content as untrusted. Do not let it write to memory or trigger actions without a check, given the Atlas and Comet attacks.
- Money actions should default to "agent prepares, user pays" (matches Accenture's 32% vs 9%).
- Logs and a visible "what I did" record address the trust gap directly.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed measurement of approval fatigue for AI agents specifically. The Anthropic numbers (93% approval; human review 13.6% vs classifier 89%) are only in secondary blogs.
- No independent failure-rate data for Atlas or Comet on real tasks.

## 4. What "advanced" features power users want next

### Takeaway
The evidence here is thin and mostly qualitative. The recurring asks are: memory you can see and control, reliable agents that finish multi-step tasks, proactive help that is useful rather than noisy, and safe use of connected accounts (email, calendar).

### Cited Findings
- Memory users want more visibility, accessibility, transparency and user control. — [UGA Grady CHI 2026](https://grady.uga.edu/research/ai-never-forgets-exploring-users-perceptions-of-ai-memory-and-chatgpts-memory-feature/)
- Pulse reviewers valued card-style morning triage and follow-ups on ongoing projects, best with memory plus Gmail/Calendar connectors. Privacy-minded users prefer on-demand use. — [SentiSight](https://www.sentisight.ai/is-sam-altman-right-about-chatgpt-pulse/); [TecKnexus](https://tecknexus.com/chatgpt-pulse-openai-proactive-ai-morning-briefs/) (commentary)
- Reviewers rate AI browsers on multi-step reliability, cross-tab context and voice dictation (Comet rated more reliable but slower than Atlas in one review). — [Best AI Browsers 2026](https://www.web3aiblog.com/blog/best-ai-browsers-2026) (single review)
- Long-tenure users get better results (Anthropic, March 2026), suggesting power users value learned workflows and context. — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/experienced-claude-users-succeed-10-more-and-the-gap-is-widening/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Likely high-value "next" features for a one-user assistant: a memory inspector, a steerable daily brief, reusable skills/workflows, and research-and-compare agents with a confirm-to-act step.

### Gaps
- No systematic survey of power-user feature requests (forums, feedback boards) was found in the time available. Reddit/HN threads were not sampled.
